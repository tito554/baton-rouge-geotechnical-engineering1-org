Sending structural fill to the site without a complete grain size distribution is a gamble no contractor in Baton Rouge should take. We have seen imported material rejected by the geotechnical inspector because the fines content exceeded the spec by less than two percent. A simple oversight that stops the compaction crew and adds demurrage to the trucking bill. Our lab runs the full curve—sieve stack plus hydrometer—following ASTM D2487. For projects near the Mississippi River alluvium where silts and clays dominate, we often pair this with an Atterberg limits test to nail the USCS group symbol before the material hits the fill lift. Same-day reporting is standard when samples arrive before 10 a.m.

You cannot classify a silty sand from a clayey sand by sieve alone—the hydrometer draws the line where the eye cannot.

How we work

The Pleistocene terrace deposits underlying the higher ground east of downtown Baton Rouge sit on formations that can shift between clean sands and fat clays within a single boring. That variability makes the hydrometer portion of the test non-negotiable. We wash the minus No. 200 fraction through a 75-micron sieve, then run a sedimentation analysis using a 152H hydrometer calibrated with sodium hexametaphosphate as dispersant. The combined curve gives the D10, D30, and D60 needed for the coefficient of uniformity and gradation. For fill compaction control on DOTD projects, the gradation parameters feed directly into the acceptance criteria under the Louisiana Standard Specifications for Roads and Bridges.

Site-specific factors

A commercial building pad off Siegen Lane was pre-loaded with borrowed fill that passed the visual classification on site. No lab gradation was run. After the first heavy summer rain, differential settlement cracked the slab-on-grade across the east wing. We pulled samples and found the fill was a gap-graded silty sand with 22 percent passing the No. 200—material that pumps water under repeated loading. The repair meant undercutting 18 inches and recompacting with select fill that met a controlled gradation envelope. The hydrometer curve we ran post-failure showed the fines fraction was plastic silt, not rock flour. That single parameter changed the drainage behavior entirely. When Louisiana humidity keeps the subgrade near saturation for months, a missing D60 can become a six-figure floor repair.

Quick answers

How long does a full sieve plus hydrometer test take in your Baton Rouge lab?

Standard turnaround is three working days. The hydrometer portion requires a 24-hour sedimentation period by ASTM D7928, so same-day results are not possible for the full curve. If you only need the sieve portion down to the No. 200, we can report that within one day. We offer a 24-hour rush on the combined test for an additional fee.

What is the cost for a grain size analysis in Baton Rouge?

A standard sieve plus hydrometer analysis runs between US$110 and US$180 per sample, depending on whether we need to run the hydrometer on the full minus-No.200 fraction or a split. Volume discounts apply for five or more samples from the same project.

How much sample material do you need for a complete gradation test?

We need at least 500 grams of dry material for sandy soils and a minimum of 200 grams for fine-grained soils. If the sample is wet when it arrives, send roughly double that mass. We oven-dry everything before splitting, so extra material covers the moisture loss.

Can you run the test on soil with gravel larger than 3 inches?

Yes, but the oversize fraction requires a field measurement of the total mass and maximum particle size before the lab split. We follow ASTM D2487 procedures for cobble and gravel corrections and note the percent oversize on the report. The gradation curve is plotted on the minus-3-inch fraction unless otherwise requested.

Do you pick up samples from job sites around Baton Rouge?

We provide sample pickup within a 25-mile radius of our lab, covering most of East Baton Rouge Parish and parts of Livingston and Ascension. Call before noon and we will have a runner at your site the same afternoon. Samples can also be dropped off at our receiving window until 5 p.m. weekdays.
